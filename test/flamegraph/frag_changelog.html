Fifty-two changes since the b394ae5 baseline — forty-eight byte-identical (thirteen from one overnight bit-equal sweep) (90/90 move-parity, zero strength risk) and four behavioral (Lemire rnd, playout throw-in, latent widening, false-eye test), each gauntleted over 1000 paired GnuGo L0 games — all measured on the ProjectABE cycle bench; a closing flash-reclaim hunt then freed 1,214 B (final entry), trading +1.59% midgame for the headroom:

  1. Liberty-flood restructure. groupLibsCore floods from the seed's neighbours instead of re-scanning the seed. −0.66%
  2. Keima pos+offset. The cuttable-keima scan in candidatePrior reads its fixed shape as simBoard[pos+offset], dropping the per-access Y×9 index multiplies and the waist coordinate math. −0.52%, −106 B
  3. Edge pos+offset. The same trick on pattern3Index's edge branch (≈40% of a 9×9's cells). −0.28%
  4. isqrt32 byte-span normalization. Jump the initial bit straight to the top power-of-4 of the input's high byte instead of shifting down from 230. −1.04%
  5. UCB confidence-sqrt skip. The exploration variance min(¼, q(1−q) + sqrt(2·lnN/n )) reaches the ¼ cap from the confidence term alone for every child with n ≤ lnN/128 — the large majority in a 400-iteration tree — so its isqrt is provably redundant and skipped. −3.87% — the session's biggest single win.
  6. Keima knight-offset table. Iterate the 8 knight offsets straight from a PROGMEM table instead of filtering a 5×5 box (25 cells) via a*a+b*b==5. −4.14%, −116 B
  7. isqrt32 at -O2. -Os emits 32-bit shifts as loops (~13 instructions to shift a single-bit value down by 2); __attribute__((optimize("O2"))) unrolls them inline while the rest of the firmware stays -Os. −1.23%
  8. -O2 sweep. The same per-function -O2 across four more shift/arithmetic-heavy hot functions — buildNearMask, playoutTry, patternBonus, hasLiberty. Same algorithm, only codegen. −3.44%
  9. Fused lpm Z+. avr-libc's pgm_read_byte(p++) compiles to lpm plus a separate adiw pointer bump; lpm Z+ fuses the read and post-increment into one 3-cycle instruction. Routed through every neighbour walk (~20 flood/scan sites + hasLiberty) — the engine's single hottest line, one instruction where there were two. −2.08%, −148 B
  10. Win-rate restoring divide. The Q6 win rate (nWins<<6)/nv has quotient ≤ 64 — a child can't win more games than it played — so a 7-step restoring divide replaces libgcc's full 16-bit __udivmodhi4. −1.14%
  11. RAVE-ratio restoring divide. (K<<12)/(3n+K) — the only hot 32-bit divide — factors exactly to 409600/(n+100) (1228800 = 3·409600, so the floors match), dropping its quotient to 12 bits for a 12-step divide that deletes __udivmodsi4 entirely. −1.40%
  12. Constant rnd() moduli. The caller-breakdown of the last hot divide (__udivmodhi4) showed 70% is rnd16() % n, much of it constant n (rnd(3), rnd(81)). At -Os gcc emits a small rcall __udivmodhi4 even for a constant divisor; a -O2 template helper folds % N to a magic-multiply. Byte-identical. −2.33%
  13. Lemire rnd() reduction. The variable sites (rnd(nl), rnd(nMatches)) can't constant-fold, so map the draw with (rnd16()·n)>>16 — a multiply, no divide. Not byte-identical (a different draw→index map), but the same uniform distribution and validated strength-neutral (1000 games vs L0, McNemar p=0.83). Together the two rnd() changes delete 70% of __udivmodhi4 (4.9% → 1.5%). −1.20%
  14. Two-phase circular scan. The random-start board scans (playout's global probe, widenNode's candidate scan) computed pos = start+i with a per-cell wrap check — the single hottest inlined line. Walking the same order in two phases (start..80, then 0..start−1) makes the loop variable be pos and runs the wrap logic once. −1.09%, −8 B
  15. isqrt32 bookkeeping. The span tests x & 0xFF000000 materialised 32-bit masked temps (movw/eor/or chains) — byte extraction (uint8_t)(x>>24) is a register rename and a single tst; and the refine loop's 4-byte bit != 0 compare became a dec/brne counter (the iteration count is knowable at entry). Verified by a 67.4M-input fuzz. −0.40%, −38 B
  16. Bitmap mask table. Every bitmap site — widenNode's have/near filters (its two hottest lines), mctsIterate's per-cell RAVE fold, raveMark, regionDone — computed 1 << (i&7) per access, an -Os variable shift loop. An 8-byte PROGMEM mask table makes it one lpm. Found by reading the book-walker's asm. −0.49%, −52 B
  17. isFar gates in candidatePrior. A far candidate (no stone within Chebyshev 2) can't have a keima partner, a diagonal friend, a non-empty 3×3 pattern or a near enemy — yet all four blocks ran their probes anyway. Gating them is byte-identical and phase-shaped: −2.3% on opening thinks (the 600-iteration boosted moves, the human-wait peaks) and a wash at midgame — which is why the headline bench number above doesn't move. −2.3% opening
  18. Latent batch widening. widenNode — the profile's #1 bar — rescanned all 81 cells with full prior evaluation for EVERY candidate the widening schedule admitted (~780 scans/think into a 212-node pool). One scan now yields the top three: the best joins the tree immediately, the others wait as latent children (move bit 7; invisible to selection) and activate by flag-flip when the visit schedule reaches their slot — scans ÷3, schedule semantics byte-exact (verified at the root). Plain batching without the latency measured −2.4pp: early arrivals dilute UCB evidence, and the stingy schedule turns out to be load-bearing. Gauntlet: 161 v 165 /1000, p=0.86 — neutral. En route: a three-link memory-corruption bug ending in the recycler freeing the ROOT (whose 0xFF move sentinel matches the latent bit test) — fixed by active-first allocation, prior-recovery activation and a latent-skipping selection fallback. −5.0%
  19. Keima companion tables. KEIMA_L[ki] = a+9b stores each knight/jump partner as one linear offset (no b×9 multiply per entry); L/2 is the jump midpoint in both branches, L−a recovers the box corner, and KEIMA_W1/W2 replace the waist-selection branch with per-entry constants. −0.31%
  20. Packed-return regionVital. Returns (vital<<8)|owner instead of writing through an out-param pointer — the hot playout caller drops its stack slot, and candidatePrior's cache-only call needs no dummy local. −0.17%
  21. groupLibsCore flood-stack direct push. Read straight from the disassembly: the seed fast path buffered same-colour neighbours in a stack-frame same[4], then a second loop copied them into the flood stack while stamping marks. Pushing directly (dead scratch on early return, table order preserved) kills the copy loop and the function's only stack frame — the cheaper prologue lands on every call including the early-return fast path. −0.43%
  22. playout at -O2. The banked lever from the rnd() work, finally affordable: the vendored-USB flash strip (−1.8 KB) paid for the +764 B of unrolled shifts and arithmetic. Byte-identical. −1.77%
  23. Playout throw-in exception. The playout policy banned all self-atari, so rollouts could never play the sacrifice that kills an eyespaced-dead group — dead groups survived and the eval read a true −16.5 board at ~50%. Allowing exactly the throw-in shape (a lone stone in self-atari — by construction surrounded by enemies) fixes ~11pp of the calibration bias, gauntlets strength-neutral (+0.5pp, p=0.81)… and is the largest single speed win of the session: rollouts that can kill dead groups resolve — captures accumulate, the mercy rule fires early, fewer playouts limp to the move cap. −10.8%
  24. Packed-return playoutTry. ko travels by value and the result packs legality + new ko into one uint16_t (0 fail / 0x100|ko) — two pointer derefs, two indirect stores and the caller-side address plumbing gone from a ~110K-call path. −2.0%, −2.2% opening
  25. Packed-return groupLibsCore. The flood core returns count | l1<<8 | l2<<16 in one uint32_t; the four wrappers unpack by byte extract. Smaller win than playoutTry — the tax is per-call fixed overhead and the flood body dominates here. Closes the packed-return audit: the idiom pays only across true call boundaries (pattern3Index, inlined, measured +0.6% and was reverted). −0.42%, −60 B
  26. Fused simPlay neighbour walk. The placed stone's neighbourhood was walked up to three times — capture check, self-atari classification, ko detection. One pass now gathers everything: the classification tallies are consumed only when nothing was captured (exactly when the board was unchanged mid-walk), and for a single-stone capture the captured cell is the new stone's extra liberty, so the old third walk reduces to !connected && e==0. −0.9%, −1.0% opening, −42 B
  27. simPlay register-pressure barrier. Read from the prologue: &simBoard[pos] was CSE'd into a call-saved pair and held across every hasLiberty call just for the rare reject-path undo store. An empty asm barrier on pos forces a 4-cycle recompute there instead; the __prologue_saves__ entry drops from 15 saved registers to 13. (The tempting leaf/slow split measured +2.3% — GCC framed the leaf and killed the tail-call — and is recorded dead.) −0.26%
  28. playoutTry register-pressure barrier. Same read, same cure: pos was saved twice across the simPlay call — a byte copy plus a zero-extended pair kept alive for the inlined raveMark's bitmap address. One barrier after the call and GCC re-derives the address, packing pos/toMove as single bytes: 5 pushes become 3, and the freed pair improves the body codegen beyond the push/pop arithmetic. −0.86%, −0.98% opening
  29. patternBonus line fixes. The 2-bit weight-code extract >> ((n&3)*2) compiled to a variable-shift loop (no barrel shifter) — two conditional constant shifts are fixed cost; and the edge loop's row bound, invariant per row, hoisted out of the per-cell test. −0.36%, −4 B
  30. pattern3Edge out of line. patternBonus is a leaf yet paid a 9-register trampoline frame — the edge-class loop's x/y counters and 16-bit mult/idx pairs inflated the union save set. The edge branch moved to a noinline packed-return helper with its own frame; the interior mirror-fold majority now enters with 2 pushes. (The same split shape REGRESSED +2.3% on simPlay — it works iff the hot path's working set fits in call-clobbered registers.) −0.69%, +52 B
  31. Keima bounds mask. The cuttable-keima loop ran two PROGMEM loads and four signed compares per entry just to bounds-check the partner; bit ki of KEIMA_MX[x] & KEIMA_MY[y] is that predicate for all 12 at once, and KEIMA_B leaves the loop. Plus the second-line 5×5 enemy scan as a row-pointer walk. −0.43%, +36 B
  32. 8-bit bitmap shifts. pos >> 3 feeding array indexing promoted to a 16-bit asr/ror dec/brne loop (~14 cycles) at -Os; explicit uint8_t locals make it three lsr, shared across the have/near tests. −0.04%
  33. Mirror-fold combine by table. M*378 + L*27 − L(L−1)/2 ran three 16-bit multiplies on every interior pattern call — both nonlinear terms have tiny domains (M≤8, L≤26), so two small PROGMEM word tables replace the arithmetic. −0.31%, +72 B
  34. 3×3 sequential pointer walk. The interior fold's 8 neighbour reads each paid ~5 cycles of address arithmetic; one pointer walked in scan order loads row-adjacent cells with 2-cycle ld Z+. −0.11%, −14 B
  35. boardAt: constant-high-byte addressing. A board index (≤80) added to lo8(simBoard) cannot carry — asserted at build time in checkmagic.sh — so &simBoard[q] is 3 instructions with a link-time-constant high byte, and the freed high-byte temp improves allocation through both flood inner loops (hasLiberty + groupLibsCore). −1.66%, −16 B
  36. boardAt batch 2. Six more provably-in-range sites: simPlay's fused walk, isOwnEye, playoutTry's guard, the playout scans, widenNode's filter. (Batch 3 — the complex scan-side loops — measured +5.9%: the d-register constraint causes spill cascades under pressure. Tight loops only.) −0.72%, −18 B
  37. False-eye test — hunt cycle 10. The blunder hunt's dominant close-game class traced to one mechanism: a connection point whose four orthogonal neighbours are own stones read as an eye under the crude all-orthogonals rule, making it unplayable for widen admission and the playouts — in the diagnosed game the winning move held a +19 prior and never entered the tree. michi's diagonal refinement (enemy on 2+ diagonals interior, 1+ at the edge = false eye, fillable) fixes admission and lets rollouts resolve fragmented-group life and death: root eval on the blunder position flipped 28%→77%. Gauntlet +2.5pp (190v165, p=0.16, the strongest positive lean of the session); opening thinks −10.4% as a side effect — playouts that can fill false eyes resolve instead of limping to the move cap. Midgame pays +1.4% for the diagonal reads. +2.5pp, −10.4% opening, +102 B
  38. markPtr: the boardAt trick's sibling. Initially dismissed on a botched carry calculation — board indices are ≤80 and lo8(simMark)+80 cannot carry in either build (checkmagic-asserted). Marks are read and written per flood iteration; applied to the two proven-safe loops only, per the batch-3 spill lesson. −0.27%, −0.33% opening
  39. Root RAVE β by table. β = isqrt32(raveRatio(nv) << 12) is a pure function of the child's visit count, yet the blend recomputed a 12-step restoring divide plus an isqrt for every root child on every selection. A 64-entry PROGMEM table of exact integer-pipeline values covers nv < 64 — where root children spend nearly all their selections — and turns out to have been carrying most of isqrt32's entire ~7% think share. The second-largest single win in the project's history, behind only the throw-in. −6.9%, −5.3% opening, +128 B
  40. The overnight sweep (27 hunt→verify→commit loops, 13 survivors). All byte-identical, each gated on movecmp + a clean two-phase bench, regressions reverted on the spot: hasLiberty takes its colour by parameter and pointer-walks its flood stack; regionVital and the playout policy pick up the boardAt sites the batch-3 revert threw out with the bathwater (buildChainMap confirmed as the actual poison at +8.5% solo — its loads are now marked untouchable); selectChild resolves the node pool once per child (nRefVisits/nRefWins on a cached reference); posXY becomes an 81-byte PROGMEM table (−0.51%); the per-iteration board refresh unpacks 4 cells per packed byte instead of a variable-shift packedGet per cell (−0.88%); the playout pattern scan hoists its row bound and the 5×5 support scan walks a row pointer; the keima loop terminates at its mask's last set bit; the RAVE fold byte-walks the mask, skipping 8 cells per zero byte (−0.64%); and the global probe gets a pre-checked playoutTryOpen entry (−0.35%, funded by reverting the marginal nBump micro to keep the flash floor). Net −4.7% midgame, −4.6% opening, flash +120 B against a 250 B reserve, ten designs measured dead and fenced in the log.
  41. The flash-fit campaign — and the β table it almost cost. Refitting the shipped firmware under the 28,672 B bootloader ceiling (29,022 → 28,538): the big lever was relocating nnOpeningMove’s 72 B of scratch below AVR’s 63 B ldd Y+n frame limit (−316 B, a threshold effect), plus capArr, a candidatePrior coord-hoist, single-pass renderBoard, and the font remap table. But one trim deleted the BETA_TAB from change #137 — mis-filed as “tiny root-only” when it runs per rave-eligible root child per iteration. Re-profiling the working tree caught it: +8.9% midgame (223.1M vs 204.9M @ 400 iters), almost exactly #137’s −6.9% handed back. Restored as a 32-entry table (nv<32 catches the 16–31-visit cluster; a 16-entry table recovered nothing, 32 ≈ the full 64) for 64 B — recovers the 7.9%, movecmp-identical. Also carry-correct markPtr/chainPtr (a real device-only flood-corruption fix, +0.37%) and a runtime RAM-layout guard. Device 28,640 (32 B free); commit 4977e5f. The lesson of #137, relearned: “root-only” is not “cheap” when it is per-child-per-iteration — and the one campaign change never emu-benched was the one that regressed. regression caught & fixed, mid 206.4M/12.9 s
  42. The flash-reclaim hunt (28,640 → 27,426, −1,214 B — 1,246 B free). The dedicated hunt the closing note used to say was still owed. Value-identical levers first: a game.cpp packed-index refactor (−136 B, killed a decode→re-encode round-trip), the nnCheby recomputed-invariant (−78 B: the opening scoring loop rebuilt ly*9+lx per candidate when that is the packed last move), and a noinline sweep run both directions — removing the attribute from the once-called nn-opening helpers so inlining deletes the call machinery (−224 B, a cascade: each round drops more callees to once-called), then adding it to outline what the compiler over-inlined (rnd16 −92 B at 544 B per 1%-think — it still inlines into its hot caller rnd(), so only scattered sites pay a call; plus simPlay, five warm helpers, and two cold jaylib UI functions, all pure or net-faster). Counter-intuitively the big hot functions are the worst outline candidates (candidatePrior +1.9%/64 B = 34 B/%, rejected). One deliberate speed-for-flash trade under the rate policy: -O2 off playout, −468 B for +1.59% — that trade is why midgame reads 209.3M here, up from 206.4M, and it is paid because the freed flash funds the banked -mno-call-prologues. −1,214 B flash · +1.5% mid (traded)
